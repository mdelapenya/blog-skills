# Voice Examples

> Copy this file to `voice-examples.md` in your blog repo root and fill it with
> your own writing. `blog-writer` and `blog-editor` read it in Step 0.
>
> It lives in the blog repo, not in this skills repo and not in any agent's
> memory, so it follows you across machines and across agents.

The conventions in `blog-writer` and `blog-editor` are negative rules: no em
dashes, no walls of text, no bolding mid-sentence. They can tell an agent what
is wrong. They cannot tell it what good looks like on *your* blog.

This file is the positive half. It is a corpus, not a style guide: real lines
you wrote, and the reasoning behind the ones you rewrote.

Keep it short. Five strong examples beat thirty mediocre ones. Prune an entry
when it stops being the best example of its pattern.

## Recurring patterns

A short list of the moves that show up across your best posts. Examples of the
kind of thing that belongs here:

- Prefer a named, checkable idea over an invented analogy. A reader can verify
  a named concept; they can only nod along to a metaphor.
- The strongest line in a draft is often buried in the body, not sitting in the
  title. When titling, offer the candidates already present in the prose.
- Lead with concrete wants, not with capability. "I wanted to stop doing X" is
  a better opening than "this tool can do Y".
- When a post needs numbers, give them relative and bucketed by regime, and
  state plainly what the data cannot settle.

Replace these with yours. The list is only useful if every line came from
something you actually wrote.
